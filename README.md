You open two tabs. One drive promises 14,800MB/s. The other promises 7,300MB/s and costs far less. Your laptop, or your older motherboard, may never let either drive show what it can do.

That’s the SSD buying problem in 2026: the box shows the maximum, but your system decides what you actually get. The biggest jump most people will feel is moving from a hard drive to any SSD. Moving from a good Gen4 SSD to a Gen5 SSD is usually much smaller.

This guide is research-based. Specs come from manufacturer pages, performance observations come from independent reviews, and we haven’t run our own benchmarks.

A note on 2026 pricing. Consumer SSD prices have risen sharply because AI data centres are absorbing a large share of NAND flash supply, and industry reports suggest the pressure may last into 2027. Check live prices and compare price per TB before buying.

Our picks

Best overall: Samsung 990 PRO (PCIe 4.0 x4, up to 7,450 / 6,900MB/s). It sits near the top of what Gen4 can deliver, with strong endurance (600TBW on the 1TB model) and a five-year warranty. Gen5 drives are faster on paper, but for gaming and creative work the gap is usually smaller than the spec sheet implies.

Best Gen5: Samsung 9100 PRO (PCIe 5.0 x4, up to 14,800 / 13,400MB/s). Roughly double the 990 PRO’s rated sequential read, up to 8TB. It only pays off with a Gen5 slot, a fast source or destination, and good cooling. It runs warmer and draws more power.

Best for gaming: WD_BLACK SN850X (up to 7,300MB/s read). Reviewers praise it under long, heavy workloads. A heatsink version is the one listed for PS5. Game load times depend heavily on your CPU, memory and the game itself, so a faster drive doesn’t mean proportionally faster load screens.

Best budget NVMe: WD Blue SN580 (up to 4,150MB/s). Low power and a single-sided design. It’s DRAM-less, so sustained writes slow once the cache fills, which rarely matters for booting and everyday use.

Best laptop upgrade: Samsung 990 EVO Plus (PCIe 4.0 x4 / 5.0 x2). High speed with better efficiency, suited to thin laptops, mini PCs and handhelds.

Best portable: SanDisk Extreme PRO USB4 (up to 3,800 / 3,700MB/s). Rugged and IP65-rated. One reviewer measured about 3,170MB/s read and 2,860MB/s write, well ahead of 20Gbps USB drives, but only on a USB4 or Thunderbolt port.

Best SATA: Samsung 870 EVO (up to 560 / 530MB/s). If your computer has no NVMe slot, it’s still the best way to make an older machine feel new.

Gen3 vs Gen4 vs Gen5 in one line each

Gen3: low cost and broad compatibility.
Gen4: the best balance of speed, price and heat for most people.
Gen5: worth it only for very large, frequent transfers on a true Gen5 slot.

How much storage? 1TB suits everyday use and moderate gaming. 2TB is often the sweet spot for price per TB. A 1TB drive shows about 930GB usable before formatting overhead.

Compatibility checklist:

Does your system support NVMe, SATA or both?
Is the M.2 length 2280?
What PCIe generation is the slot?
Is there laptop clearance, and a space for a heatsink if needed?
Is your data backed up?

The bottom line: for most upgrades, start with a compatible Gen4 drive like the 990 PRO or 990 EVO Plus. Choose Gen5 only if your workload and board can use it, and SATA if your system can’t take NVMe.
